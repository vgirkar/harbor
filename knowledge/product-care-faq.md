# Product care guide

## Waterproof shell jackets and trousers

Wash shells every 10 to 15 days of use or when they stop beading water. Use a technical wash (no fabric softener, no powder detergent), 30 degrees, gentle cycle, and rinse twice. Tumble dry on low for 20 minutes or iron on the lowest setting with a cloth in between; the heat reactivates the durable water repellent (DWR) finish. If water still soaks in after washing and heating, re-proof with a wash-in or spray-on DWR product. Never dry clean a membrane garment.

## Down jackets and sleeping bags

Wash down products only when needed, in a front loading machine with a down specific wash at 30 degrees. Dry in a tumble dryer on low with two or three clean tennis balls until completely dry, which can take two to three hours; damp down clumps and loses loft. Store sleeping bags uncompressed in the large storage sack, never in the stuff sack, to keep the loft.

## Backpacks

Empty the pack, brush out sand, and hand wash with lukewarm water and mild soap. Do not machine wash: it damages the foam back panel and coatings. Dry away from direct sun. Lubricate zips once a season with a silicone zip lubricant. Store the pack with the hip belt and straps loosened.

## Tents

Always dry a tent completely before storing it; mildew is the most common cause of tent damage and is not covered by warranty. Clean with water and a soft sponge, never detergent, which strips the waterproof coating. Reseal seams every two to three years with a seam sealer matched to the fly fabric (silicone for silnylon, polyurethane for PU coated fabrics). Keep poles out of sand and wipe them before packing.

## Footwear

Remove the insoles and laces, brush off mud, and wash with water and a boot cleaner. Dry at room temperature stuffed with newspaper; never on a radiator or in direct sun, which cracks leather and delaminates soles. Re-proof leather boots with a wax or cream every few weeks of use and fabric boots with a spray.

## Merino and synthetic base layers

Machine wash at 30 degrees inside out, no softener, no bleach. Lay flat or hang to dry; tumble drying shrinks merino. Pilling on merino is normal in the first weeks and can be removed with a fabric shaver.
